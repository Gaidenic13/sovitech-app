# 0041. Performance budgets (proposed)

- **Status:** Proposed
- **Date:** 2026-09-30

## Context

- **Prompt 3 section 11, "Performance budgets":** "These are initial targets proposed by this prompt, because no source sets them (PRD section 11). Record them in an ADR with status 'Proposed', citing the PRD's D id for the unset targets. Measure them on the `perf` fixture in phases 2 and 4. Report misses; a miss is reported, not a failing check." PRD section 11, "Proposed (PRD), not requirements": "Performance targets. No source sets a target … Target not set (D-22)"; prompt 3 5.2, "Performance budgets". WCAG 2.2 AA and the 1440×900 viewport are D-35's.
- Phase 2 measured the extraction budget (build log, phase 2 "Measurements"; ADR 0034) without writing this ADR; phase 3 writes it because it adds the first measured web budget.

## Decision

The initial targets, as prompt 3 section 11 proposes them, each reported (never a failing check):

1. **Uploads:** files up to 500 MB each, resumable; the wizard stays usable during upload and analysis.
2. **Extraction:** on a `perf` IFC4 file of about 100 MB, the data pass within 5 minutes and the viewer conversion within 15 minutes, each under 4 GB of memory on the development machine. Phase 2: 12.3 s and 1.19 GB (23 s and 1.80 GB in the sandbox), within budget (ADR 0034).
3. **Viewer:** first view of that model within 5 seconds; orbiting at 30 fps or better on integrated graphics with the tab under 1.5 GB; storeys on demand; code-split (phase 4). Phase 4 measured the viewer spike on 2026-10-02 (ADR 0046, "Measurements"; no viewer is in the app): the viewer conversion of the `perf` model in its sandbox took 42 to 57 s at 751 to 767 MiB (812 to 844 MiB for the whole 4 GB container), within the 15 minutes and 4 GB of item 2; the first view came in 2.95 to 3.44 s on the Apple M1 Pro's integrated GPU and in 3.93 s in software rendering (SwiftShader), within budget; orbiting ran at 60 fps (the display rate) on the integrated GPU, within budget, and at 1.0 fps in software rendering, a miss; the tab's renderer process stayed at or under 571 MiB, within budget; one storey was shown alone in 96 to 288 ms, but the whole converted file is loaded at once, so storeys are shown on demand, not loaded on demand (a miss as worded); a viewer would be its own lazily loaded chunk of about 1.03 MiB gzipped and a 0.27 MiB worker. The synthetic `perf` model repeats one floor, so a real model of its size will convert to a larger file; the integrated graphics of a typical office laptop were not measured. Each miss is reported, not a failing check.
4. **Web:** the wizard's initial JavaScript under 300 KB gzipped, measured on `vite build` output at the end of phase 3 and recorded in the build log. Phase 4 (2026-10-02, the integrator): the app is still one initial script, now with the workspace's six pages in it: 695,023 bytes, 197,478 gzipped with `gzip -9` (168,827 at the end of phase 3), within budget. No viewer is in it (ADR 0046); a viewer would be its own lazily loaded chunk. After phase 4 part B's fixes (2026-10-02, the integrator, from the e2e build): 697,119 bytes, 198,573 gzipped, within budget. Phase 5 part A (2026-10-06, the integrator): still one initial script, now with the stored proposal, its print route and Reports: 744,005 bytes, 209,091 gzipped with `gzip -9`, within budget.
5. **Registers:** the equipment register responsive at 5,000 rows, virtualised (phase 4). Phase 4 pages the register on the server, 50 rows a page with previous and next only, so no virtual list is added (ADR 0044 decision 7). Measured on 2026-10-02 over a TEST database in Testcontainers on the development machine (tests/api/workspace-equipment-scale.test.ts, 5,000 tagged TEST assets, one API process, `app.inject`, no network): the first page 202 ms (132 ms read again), page 100 114 ms, a search by tag 416 ms. Recorded as measured; no target sets what "responsive" means (D-22 open).

6. **Workspace routes and pages (phase 4; recorded, no target set: D-22).** Measured by the integrator on 2026-10-02 against the e2e stack (the TEST database in Testcontainers, the API and worker as child processes, `vite preview`; the demo project's ten documents; one Chromium on the development machine, 11 reads a route): every workspace route answered in a median of 39 to 50 ms (90th percentile at most 84 ms, worst 97 ms; `workspace`, `workspace/documents` and its delete effect, `workspace/system-scope`, `workspace/topology`, `workspace/zones`, `workspace/equipment`), the same as the wizard's step 3, step 8 and proposal views (36 to 44 ms); a workspace page opened afresh reached its readiness marker in a median of 117 ms (the proposal page) to 236 ms (Documents), worst 463 ms over five loads each. The read is the whole project state per request, as the wizard's (ADR 0044); item 5 holds its 5,000-row cost. **`projects.list`** (the project switcher's read, on every open: ADR 0043 decision 5; PRD R-145), after part B's verifier saw the e2e app shell's first list take 2,380 ms (finding V-5): each row still reads the whole project state in the project's own request (row-level security sees one project per request; ADR 0044, "Amended in part B"), now four projects at once instead of one after another. Measured on 2026-10-02 over a TEST database in Testcontainers on the development machine (one API process, `app.inject`, no network; projects created through `POST /api/projects`, each with its four step 1 answers and no document; five reads each): with 20 projects 82 to 120 ms (one after another, the same state read: 212 to 244 ms); with 100 projects 366 to 448 ms (one after another: 1,152 to 1,456 ms). A project with documents reads more per row; no target is set (D-22 open).

7. **Exports (phase 5; recorded, no target set: D-22).** The proposal PDF is printed by one headless Chromium in the API (ADR 0050 decision 2; `apps/api/src/proposal/export.ts`: one print at a time, a 256 MB JavaScript heap for the renderer, one renderer process, closed after two idle minutes and with the server). Measured by the print builder on 2026-10-05 on the development machine (the installed headless shell, build 1243; a six-page TEST print document served from 127.0.0.1; five prints in a row): the first print, the browser's launch included, 2.5 s; the next four 0.3 to 1.8 s; the browser's processes at most 330 MB resident while printing, 175 MB idle, none after it closed. The whole chain over a TEST database (G10-13: Generate, the export record, the download printed from the print route through the API's own print view, read back with pypdfium2) ran in about 9 s for two proposals of a project with nothing answered (ten A4 pages each), the TEST database's start and the browser's launch included. The print's own limit is the printer's `timeoutMs` (the API's choice; past it the download answers 503 `export_unavailable`). Measured again by the integrator on 2026-10-06 on the e2e stack (the TEST database in Testcontainers, the API and worker as child processes, `vite preview` on 127.0.0.1:4173 as `SOVITECH_WEB_ORIGIN`, requests through its `/api` proxy as the browser sends them; `scratchpad/build/p5/integrator/perf/timings-1.json`): **Generate** (`proposals.generate`, a new project with nothing answered) 230 ms the first time, then 72 to 114 ms (five presses); the stored proposal (`proposals.view`) 46 to 56 ms on the new project and 52 to 100 ms on the demo; the print view (`proposals.print`) 34 to 48 ms and 87 to 198 ms; the export record (`proposals.export`) 14 to 52 ms (one 2.3 s outlier on the demo, under the e2e run's load); the **PDF** (`exports.file`, the print route printed: 10 pages and 304,137 bytes on the new project, 365,885 bytes on the demo) 1.2 s the first time, the browser's launch included, then 0.4 s, and 0.5 to 2.4 s on the demo; Reports (`reports.list`) 41 to 141 ms; the Equipment CSV 30 to 76 ms. In the browser, a stored version opened by its address was ready about 0.7 s after its request (`proposals.view`, 270 ms). No target is set (D-22).

8. **Exports, amended in phase 5 part B (A-7; ADR 0050 decision 2, amended; recorded, no target set: D-22).** The printer's time limit (`timeoutMs`, 60 s in the app: `apps/api/src/index.ts`) now runs from the download's request, its wait in the print queue counted, where item 7's measurements started it at the print's turn; at most two prints wait behind the one printing (`maxWaiting`), and one more is refused at once (503 `export_unavailable`, reason `printer_busy`); a print whose request's connection closed is dropped while waiting and closed while running (`request_closed`). So no download waits longer than the limit, and one member's repeated downloads hold every other download for at most the limit, not a minute per queued print. Item 7's timings are unchanged: they measure prints that waited for nothing.

## Consequences

A miss is written in the build log with its measurement and cause; nothing blocks on it. The owner may set real targets when PRD section 11's are decided.

## How to reverse

Replace a target when the owner sets one; the measurements stay in the build log.
