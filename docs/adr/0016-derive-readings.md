# 0016. The derive function: readings where the guardrails leave room

- **Status:** Accepted: default, reversible
- **Date:** 2026-09-25 (amended 2026-09-25 after the phase 1 review: decisions 3, 4, 7 to 10, 15 and 16 changed, 17 to 19 added; amended again after its third round: decisions 3, 8, 9, 12, 15 and 19 changed, 20 added; amended again after its fourth round: decision 20 reverted, decisions 8, 9, 18 and 19 changed, 21 added; amended again after its fifth round: decisions 8, 10, 18, 19 and 21 changed; amended in phase 2 for NP-A of the fifth round's closing verification: decisions 8 and 10 changed; amended in the phase 2 review's fix round: decision 8 names the consequence NP-A leaves for the approver; amended in its third fix round: decision 22 added)

## Context

- **Guardrails 2.4** ("Derived, never stored"): one pure function computes the field state, the active candidate, each candidate's verification and status, whether a value is provisional and whether it is stale. **Rule 4** gives the conflict test, routing, corrections and the document-stage order; **2.3** revisions and removal; **2.5** asset identity; **rules 1, 3, 8, 9, 10 and 11** the refusals, who confirms, qualifiers and units, provisional through inputs, the engineer role and life-safety.
- **Prompt 3 section 10, phase 1:** "The one derive function: state precedence, the active candidate, provisional, stale. The conflict test over the whole spread of values. Asset identity with engineer-only merge, split and remove events." Section 5.2 defaults: no tolerance on any field; the stage order as proposed; `lifeSafety` treated as possibly life-safety until an engineer verifies the type.
- **Functions:** F-VALUE-02 to F-VALUE-08 (`docs/product/functions.md`). **D rows:** D-53 (the approver settings, the stage order among them), D-33 (the harness conventions). The owner decided no D row as of 2026-09-25, so where the text leaves room the build takes the reading below that shows more, never less, and records it here.
- The interfaces are ADR 0004's; this ADR records the readings the phase 1 implementation takes.

## Decision

1. **Facts per qualifier.** A field's eligible candidates form one fact per known qualifier. A multi-fact field (floors by level type, rooms by what they count) has `activeCandidateId` null, and each fact in `facts` names its own active candidate. A stated qualifier must be one the field registers (`FieldDefinition.qualifiers`, the registry's list, read as a registry extra; rule 8 "Qualifiers that must be stated"): any other is refused (`qualifier_unregistered`), so a misspelt basis ("Gross_Total") never forms a fact of its own that escapes rule 4's comparison. A field without the list takes no stated qualifier.
2. **An unknown qualifier is compared, never guessed** (rule 4). A value whose qualifier is absent, empty or `unknown` that matches exactly one qualified reading of its unit within tolerance gives a confirmation (`readingsToConfirm`); one that matches none, or several, is a conflict (`unqualified_matches_none`, `unqualified_matches_several`). When the field holds qualified readings but none in the value's unit, the value matches none of them (a conflict): "An unqualified value is never left unreconciled". Only a field with no qualified reading at all keeps an unqualified value as a fact of its own.
3. **Units.** `DeriveContext.unit` is the closed unit registry (2.7), required by the type. Every quantity candidate of a quantity or count field is refused (`unit_dimension`) when its unit is not in the registry, when the field's unit is not, or when the two dimensions differ; without the lookup at run time every quantity is refused. This is the read-side half of G8-4 ("A value whose unit cannot be mapped gives no quantity candidate"). Candidates of one fact in different units of one dimension are a conflict (`units_differ`); derive converts nothing and chooses no reading. Phase 5 (F-CALC-06, lossless unit conversions) decides whether a conversion is a candidate of the same field; if it is, a value and its own conversion will conflict until that is designed. The third review round asked derive to compare units of one dimension through the registry's lossless factors, so that 1.2 MW next to 1,200 kW is no conflict (and an unqualified 1.2 MW matches a qualified 1,200 kW, a confirmation). That shows fewer "Two values" labels and turns a conflict into a confirmation, which is this ADR's own example of a loosening ("a unit conversion inside derive", "How to reverse"), so it is not applied: it is proposal P-1-UNIT-FACTORS for the approver, with its diff, and a unit test pins today's reading ("units of one dimension", `field-state.test.ts`).
4. **Ambiguous readings** (rule 8: "never silently read one way"; rule 1: "Ranges need a basis"). A candidate with `alternatives` is compared by every alternative, so with another candidate it conflicts unless every reading agrees. A lone candidate whose readings differ by more than the field's tolerance is not a conflict (that would put a new question to the owner, which rule 5's test decides instead) but it is not a value either: its fact is known with `ambiguous: true` and no active candidate, so the field has none, the value is provisional, and a calculation that read it is stale. Engines read it only as a range over its readings (`range_over_options`). Alternatives that read the same value are no ambiguity. An alternative whose stated qualifier differs from the candidate's is refused (`value_shape`).
5. **The conflict test.** "The larger value" is read as |highest value|. The test runs over the whole spread (lowest to highest eligible value), with the larger of the absolute bound and the relative bound times |highest|. A tolerance counts only with a non-blank `reason` and a finite, non-negative bound; otherwise the field has none, and any difference is a conflict (prompt 3 5.2). The arithmetic is decimal on BigInt, with no binary rounding, because the domain has no dependencies (`packages/domain/src/decimal.ts`).
6. **The stage proposal** (rule 4, "Documents that disagree"). Proposed only when every candidate in conflict comes from a document whose stage is in the injected order and one tier is uniquely best and its candidates agree; `bill` is not ranked. The proposal is `proposedCandidateId` on the conflict; it never becomes the active candidate. Without an injected order nothing is proposed.
7. **Supersession** (2.3, 2.4 "Recalculation"). A candidate is superseded by a revision only when a `declared_revision_of` event by a named owner or engineer declares the new document a revision of the old one (decision 17), and the two candidates are the same fact. A `user_confirmed` or `engineer_verified` candidate is never superseded silently: the new value puts the field in conflict. A `superseded` candidate event holds only as the engine's recalculation: by the system, on a `calculated` or `estimated` candidate. Any other `superseded` event is ignored (`superseded_not_recalculation`): a revision is derived from the declared revisions, never from an event on the candidate, so no event can take one side of two disagreeing documents away (phase 1 adversarial finding, high).
8. **Verification, rejection and withdrawal count only from the right person** (rules 3, 4, 10; 2.3; rule 13). `engineer_verified` only from `sovitech_engineer` with a name; `user_confirmed` only from the owner, and only on owner or either fields; `owner_acknowledged` only from the owner. A rejection by the system is ignored; so is an owner's rejection of an `engineer_verified` candidate (rule 4: never overruled by the owner), an engineer's rejection of a `user` answer on a `decision` field (`engineer_overrules_owner_choice`: rule 3, "Choices belong to the owner"; an engineer who questions a choice leaves a note, never a rejection), and, since the fifth review round, an engineer's rejection of a candidate that is a side of an open conflict the engineer may not resolve (`resolver_not_routed`, decision 10): derive judges each such rejection against the field just before it was made, and one that took a side of a conflict routed to the owner (on a field that is not `either`) holds no more than the same engineer's `conflict_resolved` would (rule 4, "Conflicts on owner fields go to the owner"; "Only the right person's resolution closes a conflict"; rule 4 makes a rejection a resolution, "A correction is a resolution"). **A rejection is judged once, when it was made** (phase 2, NP-A of the fifth round's closing verification): the field as it stood then is the candidates created by then, the events recorded by then (verifications, so the routing then; resolutions; withdrawals; document events) and the rejections made before it, each as it was judged (`worldAt`, `madeAtOf` and the sweep in `derive`). A rejection is read as made at the later of its own time and its candidate's creation, since nobody rejects a value before it exists; rejections made at the same instant do not see each other; a record at the same instant as the rejection is in view (the strict side); a rejection whose time, or whose candidate's time, does not parse is judged last, against the field now. What arrives later leaves the judgement as it was: a rejection that held keeps its value out, so a later value forms its own comparison with the values still eligible and nobody is asked again about a value that was rejected (rule 5, "Never ask twice"; 2.4, "Eligible means not rejected"; round 5 had judged against the field now, so a rejection that held came undone when a disagreeing value arrived, verify5 probes S2, S3 and the G4-33 timing probe), and one that was refused stays refused, whatever resolution, deletion or verification follows (the author may reject again). Whether a rejection is the owner's correction, a lone rejection or the owner's note is read from the field as it stood at each point, so the owner's value entered with the rejection makes a correction from then on, and the field now reads as before. This covers one side of three as well as one of two. Outside any open conflict an engineer's rejection holds as before, including on a fact the owner confirms (P-1-OWNER-FACT-REJECTION asks the approver about that case). **The consequence this leaves for the approver** (phase 2 review, verifier finding 4): on an owner field (`confirmBy` owner), an engineer's rejection of a document's or an inference's reading made while no conflict was open holds for good, so when a reading that disagrees arrives later the field is known on the later reading, and the owner, who saw neither value, is never put the conflict rule 4 routes to them ("Conflicts on owner fields go to the owner"; "a conflict is put to someone only when values arrive without that person having seen both"); had the engineer rejected after that reading arrived, the rejection would be `resolver_not_routed` and the conflict the owner's. Which of the two the rules want depends on whether an engineer may set an owner fact aside outside a conflict, which rules 3 and 4 do not settle, and reading it either way changes who is asked (section 10: "involves fewer people"; "When unsure, treat the change as loosening"). So it is proposed, not built: P-1-OWNER-FACT-REJECTION extended to document and inference readings on owner fields (the phase 2 report), with its diff, and `field-state.test.ts` pins today's reading ("pinned until the approver decides P-1-OWNER-FACT-REJECTION for document readings (NP-A)"). On an engineer-routed conflict, or an owner-routed one on an `either` field, an engineer's rejection holds and closes the conflict as a resolution by the right person would. The owner's rejection with no value of their own is held the same way (found while fixing NP-1, its mirror): on an owner field a conflict with an `engineer_verified` side is routed to the engineer ("A conflict in which any candidate is engineer_verified goes to the engineer queue, whatever the field's confirmBy"), and the owner's lone rejection of its other side, which had closed it while the owner's `conflict_resolved` was refused, is now `resolver_not_routed`. The owner's correction (the owner's own value for the fact, with the rejection) is rule 4's own resolution ("A correction is a resolution") and holds as before; on an engineer or `for_quotation` field an owner's rejection holds only as that correction, which sends the value to the engineer queue (below), and without one it is the owner's note. On an engineer or `for_quotation` field an owner's rejection holds only as a correction: with the same owner's `user` candidate for the same fact (or with an unknown qualifier, as `planOwnerCorrection` leaves a correction typed in another unit), entered no earlier than the value it rejects, not refused or withdrawn (rule 4, "A correction is a resolution"; rule 3: there the owner acknowledges or notes, the engineer verifies). Without one it is the owner's note: the value stays eligible and goes to the engineer queue (`sovitech_will_check`, `owner_correction`). A `withdrawn` candidate event holds only when the system records the removal of every document the candidate cites, with the reason `document_erased` or `document_deleted` (the erasure function writes `document_erased`), or when a person withdraws their own `user` candidate (`by` is its `createdBy`), never the owner on an `engineer_verified` one. A candidate whose cited documents are all withdrawn or erased is withdrawn by those document events alone, and only a document event that holds removes a document (decision 21). Rejection, supersession and withdrawal events need a name (`by`).
9. **Refusals on read.** A candidate is `refused` (never eligible) when it cites a dataset with no approval record (G1-12), is estimated on a field that forbids estimation, is a `document` or `ai_inference` candidate with no evidence, is a `document` candidate with no entry whose check matched at its location (`text_match`, `ocr_match` or `region_rendered`; an `unverifiable` entry alone makes no document value: `unverified_evidence`), is an `ai_inference` whose evidence is all `unverifiable` with a confidence above low (rule 1: "Unverifiable evidence caps confidence at low"; `confidence_above_cap`), is an inferred quantity that is not a count, is calculated or estimated with no method, is calculated with no input candidate (`no_inputs`: a count of 0 over nothing is not a value, rule 1 "Zero is a value"), is estimated with no range, is calculated or estimated from a formula id and version that `DeriveContext.formulaDeclared` does not declare, or with no such lookup (`unknown_formula`: 2.1, "The calculation engine only"; 2.4, "Formula versions are immutable"; `declaredFormulaLookup`, which the app builds over the registry's declared signatures, never declares a TEST formula, prompt 3 5.4, and the test runner builds its own), is a `reference` candidate from a dataset the field's `referenceDatasets` does not list, even an approved one (`dataset_not_for_field`: section 10 adds reference datasets per field; `datasetApproved` also receives the field), is a candidate from any source but `user` on a `decision` field (`choice_not_owner`; a Suggested preselection is rendered, never a candidate), is a `user` candidate on a `decision` field whose author did not act as the owner (`choice_author_not_owner`: `Candidate.authorRole`, which the store sets from the request, is not `owner`, or is absent; rule 3, "Choices belong to the owner"; 2.1, an engineer's `user` value is "an engineer's site survey entry", a fact; rule 11, Fire Safety is the owner's opt-in; phase 1 review, round 4), fails the unit or qualifier check (decisions 1 and 3), or has a value shape that does not fit the field: a number that is not finite, a count or any of its readings that is not a whole number of zero or more (2.6 kind `count`; a shape, never a plausibility range, so no question is added), or a choice outside the options the field lists (2.4 `choice`: "enum key"; a field without the list takes any key). An estimate with no input candidates stays allowed: a stage 1 Indicative range reads benchmarks only. `formulaDeclared` is optional in the type so that callers that derive no calculated value need not pass it; a caller that omits it sees every calculated and estimated candidate refused, fewer values and never more.
10. **Resolutions** (rule 4). A `conflict_resolved` event names, by id, the candidates it covered (`coveredCandidateIds`, the ones the person was shown and compared; the store keeps them with the event as `covered_candidate_ids`) and the chosen one among them, with a name and a reason; otherwise it covers nothing (`resolution_incomplete`). "Covered" is that set, never a time window: a candidate outside it, whenever it was written, is compared again, and one created after the resolution is not covered even when named. It closes a conflict only when the right person chose: owner-routed conflicts by the owner, or by an engineer on an `either` field; engineer-routed ones only by an engineer (`resolver_not_routed` otherwise); never the system (`resolution_by_system`). The same test (`mayResolve`) decides an engineer's rejection of a side of an open conflict, against the conflicts open when the rejection was made (decision 8; fifth review round, NP-1 of round 4's closing verification: an engineer's rejection had closed an owner-routed conflict that the same engineer's resolution could not; G4-33; phase 2, NP-A). Resolutions apply to an unqualified value's conflict too: choosing a qualified reading sets the unqualified value aside with that reading's fact; choosing the unqualified value keeps it as a fact of its own, but only while every reading it was compared with is in the covered set. On engineer and `for_quotation` fields, document values the owner set aside go to the engineer queue (`review.list: 'sovitech_will_check'`).
11. **Not applicable** comes only from a named owner or engineer with a reason, or from a registry condition whose based-on field states are known without conflict (2.4). Absence never sets it.
12. **Pending** means more `analysis_started` than `analysis_finished` events, counting only events from the system with a name: code, not a person, records analysis (rule 12), so an owner or an engineer can neither start a run that never ends nor end one the system started (`analysis_not_system`). **Skipped** counts only a `skipped` event from the owner with a name (2.4: "The owner chose Skip for now"; rule 7, "Skip means skip"); one from the system or an engineer is refused (`skip_not_owner`), so it never stops a question from being asked.
13. **Provisional and stale.** A missing input state reads as "not active", so the value is stale and provisional; so does an input whose fact is ambiguous. An estimated candidate with no inputs is a leaf (a calculated one is refused, decision 9); an estimate with inputs is transparent through them (G9-7).
14. **An `ai_inference` with the owner's `user_confirmed` event stays provisional** until an engineer verifies it: 2.4 lists "an `ai_inference` that is not engineer_verified" among the provisional leaves, and derive follows the letter. This bears on step 5's inline "Yes, it's a hotel" (listed for the approver; relaxing it would be a loosening).
15. **Asset identity and evidence** (2.5; 2.3). Tag normalisation is narrow: Unicode NFC, trimmed, upper case, dash variants read as "-", no space kept around `-`, `.`, `/` and `:`. Two identities for one normalised tag throw. A split brings a merged asset back into the count; a removed asset is not revived; a merge into an asset that is not active is refused. Asset events from any role other than `sovitech_engineer` are refused (`assetEventRefusal`), and the register and counts stay unchanged; the store refuses them too (ADR 0014). `deriveAssetRegister` takes the project's document statuses. An appearance carries every evidence entry the store holds for it (`AssetAppearance.evidence` is a list, one entry per locator, possibly in several documents), and it stays live while any entry cites a document that is not withdrawn or erased (2.3: "A candidate or asset with evidence from other active documents keeps that evidence"). An appearance none of whose entries is live is no longer evidence (it stays in `appearanceIds`, not in `liveAppearanceIds`), and is not listed as a possible duplicate or as waiting for an identity; an appearance with no entry at all is no evidence either. An active asset with no live appearance is not countable (`withoutLiveEvidence`): with the 2.8 line "Source document removed" and a "For you" entry when its evidence all came from removed documents (2.3), and with neither when no appearance with an entry is stored for it.
16. **Input checks.** derive throws on candidates of mixed subjects or fields and on duplicate candidate ids, rather than deriving a state from them. Times are compared to the nanosecond from their ISO text (`packages/domain/src/time.ts`), never through `Date.parse`'s milliseconds, so the store's microseconds are kept; a time that does not parse is never earlier or later than another.
17. **Declared revisions are read so a mistake can be corrected** (2.3; phase 1 adversarial finding on revision cycles). A declaration's target is the event's `revisionOf` (the store's `revision_of_document_id`), or the newer record's `supersedes` when the event carries none. A document revises one document: its latest declaration stands. On one pair of documents the later declaration replaces the earlier one, so declaring B a revision of A after A a revision of B corrects the first. Declarations that tie on time and disagree cancel each other. A declaration that closes a cycle is ignored with every declaration on the cycle, so no document of it is superseded and its values stay compared rather than all superseded and Unknown.
18. **Refused events are listed, never applied.** `FieldState.refusedEvents` lists every stored candidate or field event derive did not apply, and every document withdrawal or erasure that does not hold on a document the field's candidates cite (decision 21: `withdrawal_without_request`, `erasure_outside_function`, `by_missing`), with its reason (`EVENT_REFUSALS`), in a total order, so the caller can record it. Section 8 names no guardrail event kind for a refused event; until one is approved the list is returned and the store's own guards refuse most of these at write time (a section 8 kind is a proposal for the approver, not applied).
19. **Why each reading is the strict one.** Decisions 1, 3, 4, 7 to 10, 12, 17 and 21 only keep a value in front of a person or refuse one that broke a rule the write paths hold; none adds a question, gate, confirmation, badge or status line. The fifth round's changes keep to that: an owner-routed conflict an engineer's rejection would have closed stays the owner's "Two values" item it already was (rule 4 puts it to the owner), and decision 21 now takes nothing from engineers that 2.3 gives them. The engineer queue entry of decision 8 uses the existing `owner_correction` reason, and the ambiguous fact of decision 4 is shown by the question engine and the resolver under rule 5 and 2.8 as they stand.
20. **Reverted in the fourth review round: the project type is not read as the owner's choice.** Round 3 had keyed the owner's choice on the registry's basis too (`confirmByBasis: 'owner_choice'`), which today names the project type, the owner's step 1 answer, so a document or an inference that disagreed with it was refused and no "Two values" showed. Rule 3's list of choices does not name the project type, and rule 4 as written makes that disagreement a conflict ("Enum candidates conflict when their keys differ"; "a document analysed later disagrees with an answer the owner gave earlier"), so the reading showed fewer labels than the rules: under section 10 ("shows fewer labels"; "When unsure, treat the change as loosening") it waits for the approver's own words. `isOwnerChoice` is again `kind === 'decision'`: on the project type a document or an inference that disagrees with the owner's answer puts the field in conflict, routed to the owner, and an engineer's rejection of the owner's answer holds as on any fact the owner confirms (P-1-OWNER-FACT-REJECTION). The registry still records the basis, and registry validation and the loosening check still read it. The reading is proposal P-1-OWNER-CHOICE-BASIS (build log, phase 1), and `field-state.test.ts` pins today's behaviour ("pinned until the approver decides P-1-OWNER-CHOICE-BASIS"). On a `decision` field nothing changed: only the owner's own `user` answer is a candidate (decision 9), and an engineer's rejection of it is refused (decision 8). An `engineer_verified` event on an older owner answer on a decision still outranks the owner's later answer, and the owner's rejection of it is refused (2.4 ranks verification first; rule 4: "never overruled by the owner"); refusing that verification is listed for the approver (P-1-CHOICE-ENGINEER-EVENT), pinned by `field-state.test.ts` ("pinned until the approver decides P-1-CHOICE-ENGINEER-EVENT").
21. **A document is withdrawn by a person, and by the system only for a person's withdrawal it names; it is erased by the owner or the erasure function** (2.3, `DocumentEvent` and "Deleting a document"; rule 13, "Erasure"; rule 4; phase 1 review, rounds 4 and 5). `documentStatuses` holds:
    - a `withdrawn` document event from the owner or from an engineer (`sovitech_engineer`), with a name. 2.3's `DocumentEvent` gives `withdrawn` the roles `'owner' | 'sovitech_engineer' | 'system'`, and "Deleting a document" does not say who deletes, so refusing an engineer's own withdrawal would restrict what an engineer may do beyond the rules as written: a tightening that waits for the approver. Round 4 had held withdrawal to the owner alone; round 5 corrected that, and the owner-only rule is proposal P-1-DOCUMENT-REMOVAL-OWNER-ONLY (build log, phase 1). The store binds the role to the request: the owner is a member holding `owner`, an engineer a person holding `sovitech_engineer`, member or not (engineers read every project by role, ADR 0013);
    - a `withdrawn` document event from the system only when its `requestEventId` names, by id, a `withdrawn` event of the owner or an engineer on the same document that itself holds: the job carrying out a person's deletion. **Why a system withdrawal with no person's behind it removes nothing.** 2.3 describes deleting a document and its effect ("Deleting a document withdraws each candidate whose evidence comes only from that document"), and rule 13 describes the erasure job "When an owner deletes a document or asks for erasure"; neither describes a document, and the values only it supports, going with no person's action. Rule 4 ("Never silently overwrite"; "Checked values are never overridden silently" in 2.3) keeps a value from going silently, and a job's withdrawal on its own would take one side of a conflict away with nobody deciding (round 4, NEW-A: tender 6 against as-built 5 became known on the tender value). This is the strict reading where 2.3 leaves room (its role list names the system for every document event type), and it costs no person anything: the owner and engineers withdraw in their own name, and the job names their withdrawal. It is a reading, not the rules' words, so round 5 took back the section 7 row round 4 had indexed for it (G4-32) and keeps its proof in unit and store tests (`documents.test.ts`, `field-state.test.ts`, `packages/db/src/access.test.ts`);
    - an `erased` document event from the owner, or from the system with the reason the erasure function writes (`ERASURE_EVENT_REASON`, `document_erased`). Erasure is rule 13's audited job "When an owner deletes a document or asks for erasure", so an engineer's erased event, or a system one with another reason, removes nothing. A system erasure through the erasure function still needs no record of the owner's request: that is proposal P-1-SYSTEM-ERASURE-REQUEST.

    Every other withdrawal or erasure, and one that names nobody, removes nothing and is listed (`DocumentStatuses.refused`: `withdrawal_without_request`, `erasure_outside_function`, `by_missing`; in `FieldState.refusedEvents` as a `document` entry on the fields whose candidates cite the document), and the system's follow-up withdrawal of the value does not hold either (decision 8). The asset register reads the same statuses (decision 15). The store refuses the same writes (ADR 0014: CHECK `document_events_withdrawn_by_person_or_request`, SVX15). What a person's deletion does to a conflict routed to someone else stays open: the owner's deletion ending an engineer-routed conflict unseen by the engineer is proposal P-1-OWNER-REMOVAL-ENGINEER-CONFLICT, and an engineer's deletion ending an owner-routed conflict is part of the question whether an engineer may delete a document without the owner (build log, phase 1, "Questions for the owner", item 12); both are pinned by `field-state.test.ts`.

22. **An inference's confidence is read against the evidence that remains** (rule 3, "Confidence is set by the evidence and capped by code" and "Enforced by: Confidence caps. The cap is checked against each item's evidence check result"; 2.3, "A candidate or asset with evidence from other active documents keeps that evidence"; the phase 2 verifier's closing check, new problem "padding of an inferred choice"; case G3-18, for the integrator to index). The verifier caps an inference once, when it is written (ADR 0027), and a candidate never changes (2.4); but deleting or erasing a document removes the entries that cite it while the candidate stays on the others, and the tier may have rested on the entry that went ("hotel" inferred from a label naming the use and from a room schedule is high; from the room schedule alone at most medium, G3-17). So each eligible `DerivedCandidate` carries `confidence`, the tier the badge reads: for an `ai_inference`, the stored confidence (low when none is stored) capped by the entries it was written with and by the entries whose documents are not removed, never higher than stored, and never raised by removing a document (`derivedConfidence`, `packages/domain/src/confidence.ts`); for another source, its stored confidence (an ambiguous reading stays low). An entry's cap is read from the entry as stored: low when `unverifiable`; high when its excerpt, read on its own, names the chosen option with a mention no negation governs (`excerptNamesChoice`, the verifier's own reading); an IFC entry of the gated value path supports what that path's verifier gives it (ADR 0033), since this reading does not re-read a model's statements; otherwise medium. Several entries: low when any supports only low, else the highest. The verifier keeps an inference below high when an entry reads as naming the option only because its excerpt leaves out a negation its page writes (ADR 0027 decision 12), so reading the stored excerpt never overstates what remains; a property in `confidence.test.ts` checks that for any accepted inference and any documents deleted, derive reads at most the tier the remaining evidence would be verified with. The field lists it only on an eligible candidate (a value that is not eligible is never shown as current, 2.3), so the `DerivedCandidate` shape of every other candidate is unchanged. `planOwnerCorrection` logs `owner_corrected_inference` with this tier, the one the owner was shown. It lowers a tier from Likely to Possible and never raises one; it adds no question, gate, confirmation, badge or status line (rule 5's test reads any inference as uncertain, whatever its tier). Rule 3's calibration (a tier's wording dropping after corrections) is not built; when it is, it reads this tier.

## Consequences

- More conflicts and fewer silent choices: Truth up. The Speed cost falls only where units or several readings collide, or where documents disagree by any amount while no tolerance is approved (D-93).
- 2.3's "installed-equipment facts stay provisional until an as-built document, a nameplate photo or a site survey supports them" (existing-building and modernization projects) is not applied yet: it needs the project type and the registry's list of installed-equipment fields. Until then an `engineer_verified` value from a design-stage document reads as not provisional. Proposed for phase 2 or 4: an optional `DeriveContext.designStageProvisional` lookup (build log, "Next").
- An engineer splitting an untagged appearance into a new asset (phase 7) needs an identity form for untagged assets; `split_from` needs an existing identity today.
- Each reading is pinned by a unit test or a property in `packages/domain/src/*.test.ts`, and the guardrail cases of phase 1 cover rule 4's cases (build log, phase 1). The phase 1 review's attacks are reproduced in `field-state.test.ts` ("the phase 1 review attacks"), `documents.test.ts` (revision cycles) and `assets.test.ts` (appearances from removed documents), with properties that no system event changes a found or entered value and that a resolution never sets aside a candidate it did not cover.

## How to reverse

Each reading is one function in `packages/domain/src/field-state.ts`, `conflict.ts`, `documents.ts`, `assets.ts` or `time.ts`, pinned by its test. A reading that lets more values through or shows fewer labels (a tolerance without a reason, a guessed unqualified match, a unit conversion inside derive, an owner-settled inference read as not provisional, a wider tag normalisation) is a loosening under guardrails section 10 and needs the approver's own words; a narrower one is recorded here and listed for the approver.

- **Decision 20** (the project type as the owner's choice): applying P-1-OWNER-CHOICE-BASIS is one condition in `isOwnerChoice` (`field.confirmByBasis === OWNER_CHOICE_BASIS`), with its case and the flip of the pinned test; it shows fewer "Two values" labels, so it waits for the approver's own words.
- **Decision 21** (document removal): `removalRefusal` in `documents.ts`. Letting a system withdrawal that names no person's, or an erasure outside the erasure function, remove a document lets one side of a conflict go with nobody deciding: a loosening. Holding withdrawal to the owner alone again refuses engineers what 2.3 gives them: a tightening, proposal P-1-DOCUMENT-REMOVAL-OWNER-ONLY, waiting for the approver's own words. The erasure path is recognised by its role and its reason; if the erasure function's reason code changes, `ERASURE_EVENT_REASON` and the store's CHECK `document_events_erased_by_erasure` change with it.
- **Decisions 8 and 10** (a rejection in an open conflict): the lone rejections (an engineer's, and the owner's with no value of their own), judged in time by `worldAt`, `madeAtOf` and the sweep in `derive` (`field-state.ts`). Dropping the judgement lets an engineer close a conflict routed to the owner, or the owner one routed to the engineer (rule 4): a loosening. Judging them against the field now, as round 5 did, brings a rejected value back into a new conflict when a later value arrives (a question about a value a person rejected, against rule 5's "Never ask twice") and lets a later resolution, deletion or verification turn a refused rejection into one that holds; the properties in `field-state.test.ts` ("a rejection is judged against the field as it stood when it was made": a replay of the history in time order, a later value, and a cut at any minute) catch both.
- **`choice_author_not_owner`**: dropping it lets an engineer's own entry stand as the owner's choice (rules 3 and 11): a loosening.
- **Decision 22** (an inference's confidence against what remains): `derivedConfidence` in `confidence.ts`. Reading the stored confidence instead, or capping by all the entries a candidate was written with only, lets a tier outlive the evidence that earned it once that document is deleted or erased (G3-18): a loosening.
